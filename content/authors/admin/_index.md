Isaac Ahuvia is a postdoctoral fellow at the Center for Behavioral Intervention Technologies (CBITs) at Northwestern University and an instructor at Harvard University. His research investigates mental illness identity, the online spaces that shape it, and ways to promote healthier ways of understanding mental health and illness. His research has appeared in publications such as [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/06/health/mental-health-schools.html), [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/views/2024/03/07/many-depressed-students-dont-identify-such-opinion), and [Mad in America](https://www.madinamerica.com/2024/03/from-self-label-to-self-sabotage-identifying-with-anxiety-fuels-avoidance-behaviors/). He received his Ph.D. in Clinical Psychology from Stony Brook University, and completed his clinical internship in the VA Boston Healthcare System. Before studying in clinical psychology, Isaac studied sociology at the University of Michigan and conducted public policy research at the University of Chicago Inclusive Economy Lab.
